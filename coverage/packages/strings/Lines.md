The pinned iterator finds the next LF byte, includes it in the yielded line, and advances the captured remainder before the callback. If no LF remains, it yields the remaining bytes as the final line. The Lean theorem establishes that the emitted bytes followed by the remaining bytes reconstruct the original input for every callback budget. The precise LF boundaries, budget stops and repeated-call results are checked by differential comparison. Repeated invocation describes the pinned implementation's behavior outside the documented single-use contract. The evidence covers byte contents and the callback's continue decision. [Pinned Go source](https://github.com/golang/go/blob/go1.27.0/src/strings/iter.go#L13-L33) · [Lean model](../../../lean/GoLean/Strings/Lines.lean)

Go 1.27.0 and Lean agreed on 2,128 boundary inputs and 60,000 sampled inputs using seeds 17, 2718, and 65537. The differential comparison uses two bounded callback schedules, a drain and an exhaustion check against an independent split-after oracle. [Differential comparison](../../../difftest/strings/lines/check.go)

Go passed six independent fixtures covering empty input, consecutive LF bytes, CRLF, invalid UTF-8, and early stop followed by resumption and exhaustion. Restarting the iterator on each invocation failed five fixtures. Omitting LF from the yielded line failed three. [Reviewer control](controls/Lines/main.go)
