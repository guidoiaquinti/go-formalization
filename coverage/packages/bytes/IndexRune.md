The pinned source searches ASCII using IndexByte, decodes for RuneError,
rejects invalid targets, and searches valid non-ASCII encodings by
walking backwards from possible matching final bytes. The cutovers
change probe strategy but retain eligible starting offsets. The shared
Lean result model returns the first byte offset and represents the Go
miss sentinel by none. A valid complete UTF-8 spelling starts with a
noncontinuation byte, so a matching spelling cannot start inside another
successfully decoded rune. A failed decode advances by one. The special
RuneError recursion accounts for widths of preceding valid runes and
stops on either an invalid byte or a literal replacement character.

The author comparison independently uses Go range decoding to check
11,365 deterministic inputs and 60,000 samples with seeds 17, 2718 and
65537. The independent control checked 19 fixed cases against pinned
native Go and Lean on darwin/arm64. Cases include valid multibyte
prefixes, two possible matches, both orders of literal and malformed
RuneError, invalid targets, and repeated false final-byte candidates
followed by two matches or no match. The encoded-byte, rune-ordinal and
last-occurrence mutants disagreed in 7, 9 and 8 cases respectively.
The exact probe ordering and architecture-specific search strategy remain
outside the verified scope.
