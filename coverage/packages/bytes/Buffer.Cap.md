The pinned method returns the backing slice capacity. Advancing the read position changes the unread slice capacity without changing the backing capacity. Go reports the same backing capacity after reset or in-place compaction, and reports the newly allocated backing capacity after growth. A nil receiver triggers the runtime nil-pointer panic. The Lean capacity model proves this observation and the relation between the full and unread capacities given the consumed offset.

The direct Go and Lean comparison checked 12,290 boundary rows and 60,000 seeded rows with seeds 17, 2718 and 65537. The check infers new allocation size from a zero-offset view immediately after forced growth, then reads a prefix and verifies that the capacity remains the new allocation size. The runtime allocator policy is outside the claim.

The control passed ten fixed native and Lean fixtures, including read, truncate, reset, reslice, compaction, growth and nil receiver histories. Returning the unread slice capacity failed five fixtures, returning the unread length failed eight, and returning unused capacity failed seven.
