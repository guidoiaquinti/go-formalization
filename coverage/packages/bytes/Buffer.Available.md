Pinned Go 1.27.0 on darwin/arm64 returns the underlying capacity less the stored length. The read offset shortens both the capacity and length of the unread slice by the same amount. Lean proves that the difference is unchanged for an offset through the stored length and a stored length through the capacity. It also specifies zero spare capacity for a full buffer and a panic for a nil receiver.

The author comparison passed 12,290 boundaries and 60,000 samples from seeds 17, 2718 and 65537. The independent control checked 11 Go and Lean observations with `GOTOOLCHAIN=go1.27.0 GOWORK=off go run ./difftest/bytes/buffer-available/review`. It observed capacity and length through the returned unread slice, reconstructed the stored geometry, and included full, partially read, drained, reset, truncated, compacted, and reallocated states plus the nil receiver. Subtracting unread length from the total backing capacity failed three fixtures. Returning the unread slice capacity without subtracting unread length failed seven.
