The pinned function calls the query, then visits entries in slice order. Each score sums four separately truncated squared differences. The square is evaluated in uint32, and shifting it by two before adding limits each term to at most 2^30 − 1. A strict improvement updates the index, so a tie retains the earlier entry. A zero score exits immediately and can arise even for distinct channels. Lean models the callback outcomes, accumulated trace and uint32 arithmetic. Its general return theorem compares normally returning inputs with a strict-minimum fold. Additional theorems cover query failure, the empty palette and stopping at a zero-scoring first entry. The broader callback trace and panic behavior are checked in Go. [Pinned source](https://github.com/golang/go/blob/go1.27.0/src/image/color/color.go#L290-L339) · [Lean model](../../../../lean/GoLean/Image/Color/PaletteIndex.lean)

The oracle computes the signed channel differences and squares in a wider representation, reduces each square to uint32, then truncates and sums. Go 1.27.0 and Lean agreed on 11 boundaries and 60,000 sampled inputs on darwin/arm64, using seeds 17, 2718 and 65537. Inputs cover contract-valid premultiplied colors and synthetic callbacks returning arbitrary uint32s. The comparison checks the returned index, nil-interface and callback panic outcomes, and the ordered trace. The model abstracts callback effects beyond those observations.
