Go first checks the start of the applicable address range. The low 32 bits are checked for native IPv4 and both words are checked for all other detail classes. An address at the start returns the zero Addr. Otherwise the source subtracts one from the low word and borrows into the high word when needed. The Lean theorem proves the two words and resulting family and zone. IPv4-mapped IPv6 retains IPv6 width and does not hit the native IPv4 start check. [Pinned source](https://github.com/golang/go/blob/go1.27.0/src/net/netip/netip.go#L761-L773) · [Lean model](../../../../lean/GoLean/Net/Netip/AddrPrev.lean)

The Go driver constructs addresses through the public APIs and checks the bit length, raw bytes and zone against a bytewise decrement. Go 1.27.0 and Lean agreed on 16 boundaries and 60,000 sampled cases on darwin/arm64, using seeds 17, 2718 and 65537. The comparison includes an invalid zero receiver and zoned IPv6 at the range start. [Differential comparison](../../../../difftest/net/netip/addr-prev/check.go)
