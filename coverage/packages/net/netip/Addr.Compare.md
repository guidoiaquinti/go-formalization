Go 1.27.0 compares bit lengths first, then high and low words in unsigned order. If those keys tie, it compares zones only for IPv6. Mapped IPv6 remains in the IPv6 class. The Lean theorem characterizes the three outcomes as forward ordering, reverse ordering, and a tie. The IPv6 test comes from the left address. In the zone branch, equality of bit lengths forces the right address to have the same IPv6 class. [Pinned source](https://github.com/golang/go/blob/go1.27.0/src/net/netip/netip.go#L415-L449)

Go compares strings lexically by byte and Lean compares strings lexically by character. The differential inputs use only empty and decimal zones, where those orderings coincide. The evidence checks lexical zone order, including empty, equal, and differing zones. Arbitrary Go zone byte sequences are outside the comparison. The registered oracle uses raw big-endian bytes and the zone strings; 64 boundary pairs and 60,000 sampled pairs agreed on darwin/arm64 with seeds 17, 2718, and 65537.
