Go 1.27.0 tests whether `Compare` returns exactly -1. The Lean theorem proves the corresponding strict lexicographic relation on bit length, unsigned high and low words, and zone when the words tie for IPv6. Equal keys never sort before one another. Mapped IPv6 keeps the IPv6 bit length and participates in zone ordering. [Pinned source](https://github.com/golang/go/blob/go1.27.0/src/net/netip/netip.go#L415-L455)

Go's lexical byte order and Lean's lexical character order coincide for the empty and decimal zones used in the comparison. Arbitrary Go zone byte sequences are outside the tested domain. The registered driver invokes `Addr.Less` directly and compares it with strict order computed from the fixture family, bytes, and zone. Go and Lean agreed on 64 boundary pairs and 60,000 sampled pairs on darwin/arm64 with seeds 17, 2718, and 65537. Run `go run difftest/net/netip/addr-less/check.go` after building the registered Lean executable.
