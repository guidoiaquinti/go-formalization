I compared the pinned Go 1.27.0 implementation to the Lean wrapper and its shared Payne-Hanek reduction. For positive finite binary64 inputs below rounded π/4 the pair is the zero octant and the input word. At and above that bound the shared model implements the three-limb product, leading-zero normalization, even-octant correction and multiplication by rounded π/4. The Lean proofs cover the identity guard and constrain the octant to 0, 2, 4 or 6.

The direct same-package overlay matched exact octants and binary64 residual words for 8,286 boundaries and 60,000 samples with seeds 17, 2718 and 65537 on darwin/arm64. The 1,408-bit reference matched all octants and the largest absolute residual difference was 2.220446049250313e-16. The claim covers the documented positive finite domain. Nonpositive and nonfinite arguments remain outside scope.

The independent control compared Go and Lean with 22 fixtures from a 410-digit decimal Machin calculation. It includes the smallest positive subnormal, the rounded π/4 guard and its adjacent words, a limb boundary and the largest finite binary64 value. Forcing the octant to zero fails 14 fixtures, and reducing with binary64 remainder modulo rounded π/2 fails 15.
