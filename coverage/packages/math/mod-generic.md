I checked Go 1.27.0's private helper in src/math/mod.go. It rejects zero divisors, infinite dividends and either NaN before reducing finite magnitudes. A finite dividend with an infinite divisor passes through. Frexp and its significand comparison choose the largest power-of-two multiple of the positive divisor at most the residual. That scaling is exact. The subtraction is exact because the scaled divisor and residual are within a factor of two. The helper reapplies the dividend's sign to the result. Negative zero as an input also survives because it skips the loop and sign branches.

The Lean model represents these magnitudes in smallest-subnormal units. The theorem replaces repeated aligned subtraction by integer remainder under the same encoded branch conditions. The exact binary64 output correspondence is tested against a rational truncating-quotient oracle, and NaNs are observed by class.
