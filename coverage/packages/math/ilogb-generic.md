The private helper normalizes its input, extracts the biased exponent, subtracts 1023, and adds the normalization adjustment. For normal finite inputs this is the stored exponent minus 1023. For a subnormal with highest fraction bit k, the normalized exponent field is k+1, giving k-1074 after correction. Lean proves this relationship from its binary64 word model. The helper's finite nonzero precondition excludes public Ilogb's zero, infinity and NaN branches. [Pinned source](https://github.com/golang/go/blob/go1.27.0/src/math/logb.go#L34-L52)

Go 1.27.0 and Lean agreed on 216 boundary rows, including every subnormal leading-bit position for both signs, and 60,000 samples with seeds 17, 2718, and 65537 on darwin/arm64. The comparison invokes the private helper through a same-package bridge and uses an independent leading-bit oracle. Run `go run difftest/math/normalize/check.go` after building the registered Lean executable.
