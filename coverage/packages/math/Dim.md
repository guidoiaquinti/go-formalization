I checked Go 1.27.0's implementation in `src/math/dim.go`. It subtracts y from x, returns the positive zero literal if the result compares less than or equal to zero, and otherwise passes the binary64 result through. The comparison includes negative zero and excludes unordered NaN values. The Lean guard and both implications in its theorem agree with this branch. The theorem uses Lean Float subtraction and comparison. Correspondence with Go's primitives is checked by the differential within the registered domain.

I replayed `go run difftest/math/dim/check.go` through the normalized math runner using Go 1.27.0 on darwin/arm64. It passed 784 boundaries and 20,000 generated pairs per seed 17, 2718, and 65537. The oracle constructs the exact rational finite difference, clamps by sign, and rounds once to binary64. It handles infinity and NaN separately. Finite inputs are multiples of the smallest binary64 subnormal, so an exact positive difference cannot round to negative or positive zero. Go and Lean produce the same exact observed words or NaN classification. NaN signs and payloads are outside scope.

I ran `go run coverage/packages/math/controls/Dim/main.go`. All 11 fixed pairs passed. Testing for a positive result and returning zero otherwise wrongly swallows four NaN results, including infinity minus itself. Clamping only strictly negative differences leaks negative zero when subtracting positive zero from negative zero.
