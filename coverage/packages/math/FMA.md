I checked Go 1.27.0 src/math/fma.go. The implementation handles exceptional operands and signed zero before splitting finite significands. Their product is shifted into a joined double-word value and normalized. The source compares the high limbs to order equally scaled terms because the addend's low limb is zero. It then aligns with a sticky shift, adds or subtracts, renormalizes, handles subnormal rounding and applies ties to even. The Lean model preserves those decisions. Its kernel theorems cover NaN classification, the signed product path for a zero addend, and positive zero for matched opposite terms. The full finite pipeline is exercised by differential comparison. NaN payloads and hardware flags are outside scope.

In each run, Go and Lean agreed with an exact rational single-rounding oracle on 27,005 boundary triples and 60,000 sampled triples using seeds 17, 2718 and 65537.
