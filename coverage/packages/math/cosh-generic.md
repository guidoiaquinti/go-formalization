I checked the Go 1.27.0 private implementation against the Lean model. The model takes the magnitude before the branch, adds the reciprocal to the rounded exponential through 21, and otherwise halves the rounded exponential directly. It preserves sign symmetry and classifies zeros, infinities and NaNs as registered.

The pinned darwin/arm64 same-package comparison checked 96 boundaries and 60,000 sampled words with seeds 17, 2718 and 65537. The maximum difference from the separate 256-bit reference was one binary64 encoding step, outside 5,363 staged overflows where the result of the mathematical function remains finite.

An independent 384-bit direct even series checked 28 private native calls and 84 Lean profile observations. The native outputs matched the numerical encoding wherever the result remained finite. Applying the input sign failed 13 fixtures, omitting the reciprocal failed 12, and avoiding staged overflow failed two. At ±710 the pinned branch returns positive infinity while the independent numerical value is finite. Both simulated amd64 profiles also overflow at ±709.75.
