I compared the pinned Go 1.27.0 generic implementation with the Lean word model. It preserves both rational chains for small arguments, the staged reciprocal subtraction in the middle, and halving after the rounded Exp for large magnitudes. The model preserves zero signs and sign restoration, and classifies NaNs.

The native darwin/arm64 comparison matched exact outputs on 90 boundaries and 60,000 samples with seeds 17, 2718 and 65537. The 256-bit numerical reference differed by at most three binary64 encoding steps outside 5,897 observed cases where the mathematical result was finite and Go and Lean both returned infinity because Exp overflowed first. Native amd64 and the s390x dispatch remain unverified locally.

The independent control passed 24 native fixtures and 120 Lean profile rows. Signed infinities at ±710 agree with the pin and differ from the finite values computed with 110-digit arithmetic. The control also distinguishes the known amd64 Exp exponent overflow at 709.75 from the finite darwin/arm64 result, based on the pinned amd64 assembly. Losing the sign failed five fixtures, omitting the half factor failed four, and replacing pinned overflow with the mathematical reference failed four.
