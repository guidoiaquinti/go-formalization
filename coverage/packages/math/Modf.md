I checked the pinned Go 1.27.0 source in `src/math/modf.go`. The integer part is `Trunc` of the input. For finite inputs below magnitude one it is signed zero, so the fraction retains the input. At larger finite magnitudes the input and integer part have the same sign and are within a factor of two, making their subtraction exact. For fractional inputs in that range the low significand bits are the exact residue in units of the input's least significant bit. The model normalizes this residue and retains the sign as the Go `Copysign` does. Integral finite values return a signed zero fraction. An infinite integer part is preserved and its subtraction gives a NaN fraction. NaNs produce two NaNs. Native payload and NaN sign are outside scope.

I ran the normalized `math` comparison replay on Go 1.27.0, darwin/arm64. Both `go run difftest/math/modf/check.go` and the replay with intrinsics disabled in the driver and math package agreed with the exact rational oracle on 672 boundaries and 20,000 cases per seed 17, 2718, and 65537. Finite outputs and infinities are compared by exact bits and NaNs by classification.

I ran `go run coverage/packages/math/controls/Modf/main.go` in both profiles. Each passed ten fixed cases. Removing the fraction's sign copy fails on two signed-zero fractions, including for input -2. Using floor for the integer part also fails twice, including -1.25, whose integer part is -1.
