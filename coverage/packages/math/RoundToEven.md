I checked Go 1.27.0's generic `math.RoundToEven` in `src/math/floor.go` against `roundToEvenWord` and `roundToEvenWord_spec`. Below magnitude one, the source keeps the sign and produces signed one only above the midpoint. For biased exponents 1023 through 1074, the bias is half a quantum minus one when the truncated integer is even, and half a quantum when it is odd. At exponent 1023 the low bit of the exponent encodes the odd truncated magnitude one. At larger fractional exponents the sign, exponent, and implicit leading one contribute even multiples of the quantum, leaving parity to the stored significand. The proof checks that adding the bias stays within 64 bits. For exponents 1075 and above, Go's unsigned shift counts clear both the bias and the mask, so the generic path preserves the input word.

Both passed 1,086 boundaries and 20,000 generated inputs each for seeds 17, 2718 and 65537. The driver compares a numeric truncation-and-parity oracle with the theorem's quotient-and-residue formula.
