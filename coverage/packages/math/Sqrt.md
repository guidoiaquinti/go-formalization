I checked Go 1.27.0's generic moving-bit loop in `src/math/sqrt.go`. Special values return before normalization. For a positive finite input, leading-bit normalization and an odd-exponent adjustment give a radicand below (2^{54}). The loop maintains the square of the accumulated root plus the scaled residual. Its ceiling invariant bounds the residual at every digit by four times the root prefix plus four times the moving bit. The root prefix is below (2^{54}), and the moving bit is at most (2^{53}), so these and the other intermediates fit uint64. The proof gives the integer square root of the scaled radicand and its exact remainder.

The extra low digit determines rounding. An even integer square root before discarding that digit is below the halfway point. An odd one with nonzero remainder is above it. The exact halfway case would require an odd square equal to a multiple of (2^{54}), which is impossible. The final addition assembles the biased exponent and significand. The smallest positive input has a normal square root. Native NaN signs and payloads are outside scope.

I ran `go run difftest/math/sqrt/check.go` in the isolated review checkout on Go 1.27.0, darwin/arm64, and repeated it with intrinsics disabled in the driver and math package. Both passed 12,612 boundary inputs and 20,000 samples per seed 17, 2718, and 65537. The oracle uses the nearest-even math/big Float calculation and observes exact non-NaN words. Its Newton implementation starts from a native square-root estimate and refines that estimate at higher precision.

I ran `go run coverage/packages/math/controls/Sqrt/main.go` in both compiler profiles. Both passed 11 fixed fixtures. An exact rational comparison of the rounded result's square with the input identifies where a round-down mutant would step to the next smaller float. It differs for inputs 2 and 8. A zero-canonicalizing mutant loses the sign for negative zero.
