I checked the pinned Go 1.27.0 implementation in `src/math/fma.go` and its dependency on `math/bits.LeadingZeros64`. The helper counts the high word first and adds the low-word count only when the high word is zero. Each count is at most 64, so both the conversion to int32 and the addition are exact. The Lean model follows these branches. Its theorem characterizes the width of the joined unsigned word by the minimum exponent whose power of two exceeds it, with width zero for an all-zero input.

I ran `go run difftest/math/lz/check.go` on Go 1.27.0, darwin/arm64. The test overlay calls the actual private helper in the installed math package. Go and Lean matched an independent descending bit scan for 257 boundaries and 20,000 generated pairs per seed 17, 2718, and 65537. The boundaries place a highest set bit at every position in each word and include the all-zero case. The generated inputs exercise the branch where the high word is zero and vary leading zero counts in both words.

I ran `go run difftest/math/lz/review/main.go`. The control reuses the same-package bridge and test fixture. It asserts all seven fixed input/output pairs against Go. A mutant that reverses high and low words disagrees on six pairs, including high=1 and low=0. A mutant that omits the 64-bit offset when the high word is zero disagrees on three pairs, including the all-zero input.
