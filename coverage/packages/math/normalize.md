For a finite nonzero binary64 value, the pinned helper compares the absolute value with 2^-1022. A normal value is returned unchanged with adjustment zero. A subnormal is scaled by 2^52, an exact multiplication that shifts its leading fraction bit into the implicit bit of a normal value. The Lean theorem verifies the resulting sign, biased exponent and shifted fraction bits and adjustment -52. The multiplication correspondence is checked by bit-level arithmetic and tested against native Go floating-point execution. Signed zero, infinities and NaNs are outside the registered finite nonzero claim. In particular, the Go helper scales zero to zero and reports -52. The subnormal payload construction requires a nonzero fraction. [Pinned source](https://github.com/golang/go/blob/go1.27.0/src/math/bits.go#L59-L67)
