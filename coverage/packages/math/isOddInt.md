I checked Go 1.27.0's `isOddInt` in `src/math/pow.go` and `Modf` in `src/math/modf.go`. The magnitude guard rejects finite inputs at or above (2^{53}) and both infinities before integer conversion. NaNs fail the fractional-zero condition and therefore never reach that conversion. Magnitudes below one truncate to signed zero, whose integer parity is even. In the remaining finite range the subtraction from the truncated value is exact, so a zero fraction means the original encoding is unchanged by truncation. Every truncated value in this range fits int64, and the low bit has the same parity for either sign. The model preserves these tests and the theorem expresses them as exponent bounds, zero remainder, and odd quotient of the unsigned significand.

I ran `go run difftest/math/is-odd-int/check.go` on Go 1.27.0, darwin/arm64. The overlay calls the private helper using raw binary64 words. Go and Lean matched the exact rational oracle on 470 boundaries and 20,000 samples per seed 17, 2718, and 65537.

I ran `go run difftest/math/is-odd-int/review/main.go`. The existing overlay passed 13 fixed cases. Dropping the fractional-zero condition fails on three inputs, including 1.5 and -3.5. A mutant rejecting magnitudes at or above (2^{52}) fails on two representable odd integers, including (2^{52}+1).
