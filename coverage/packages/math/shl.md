I checked Go 1.27.0's helper in `src/math/fma.go`. The output low word is the original low word shifted left. The output high word combines the shifted high word with bits from the low word. Below count 64, only the rightward carry into the high word can contribute. Above 64, only the low word shifted left by the excess can contribute. At count 64 the two carry terms both copy the same low word, which bitwise OR preserves. On the tested uint64 profile, a wrapped `64-n` is at least 65 and a wrapped `n-64` is at least (2^{64}-64). Both shifts therefore contribute zero when they wrap. All terms are zero at counts 128 and above. The model preserves the remaining expressions and the theorem checks their concatenation bit by bit against a single fixed-width 128-bit left shift.

I ran `go run difftest/math/shl/check.go` on Go 1.27.0, darwin/arm64. The overlay calls the private Go helper directly. The independent math/big oracle shifts the concatenation and keeps its low 128 bits. Go and Lean agreed on 400 boundaries and 20,000 cases per seed 17, 2718, and 65537. Counts include 0, the transitions around 64 and 128, and maximum native uint.

I ran `go run difftest/math/shl/review/main.go`. The overlay control asserts eight native results. The independent-limb shift mutant fails five cases, including a shift by one that carries the low word's highest bit into the high word. Masking the shift modulo 128 fails at counts 128 and maximum uint.
